\section{Text Preprocessing}

We applied a shared pipeline in \texttt{src/preprocess.py}: lowercasing,
whitespace normalization, NLTK \texttt{word\_tokenize}, removal of pure
punctuation tokens, and English stopword filtering.
Numeric tokens (e.g., percentages and currency amounts) were kept because they
are informative in financial headlines.
Table~\ref{tab:preprocessing} compares three variants on the training set;
stopword removal shortened documents from 20.4 to 13.4 tokens on average while
barely reducing vocabulary size.
Example: \emph{``The major breweries increased their domestic beer sales by 4.5
per cent last year.''} $\rightarrow$ \emph{``major breweries increased domestic
beer sales 4.5 per cent last year''}.

\section{Classical Sentiment Classification}

We trained Multinomial Naive Bayes and a feed-forward neural network
(\texttt{MLPClassifier}, two hidden layers of 128 and 64 units) on bag-of-words
and TF-IDF features built from the Task~2 preprocessing pipeline.
For TF-IDF with Naive Bayes, down-weighting frequent tokens hurt minority-class
detection: the model collapsed toward \emph{neutral}, with negative recall
$\approx$0.01 in the multiclass setting.
BoW features worked better for NB; the best multiclass macro F1 was
0.67 (feed-forward NN + BoW), and the best binary macro F1 was 0.80 (NB + BoW).

Error analysis revealed recurring failure modes.
Subtle positive statements without explicit sentiment words were often predicted
as \emph{neutral} or \emph{negative}, e.g.,
\emph{``diesel margin remained high''} (true: positive).
Factual headlines reporting legal or financial outcomes were also misread, e.g.,
\emph{``paper maker stora enso oyj said friday acquitted charges \ldots''}
(true: positive, predicted: negative).
Headlines dominated by numbers could flip neutral/negative labels, e.g.,
\emph{``non-recurring items eur 177mn profit amounted eur 20mn''}
(true: neutral, predicted: negative).

\section{Transformer Fine-tuning}

We fine-tuned \texttt{distilbert-base-uncased} on the same train/test split as
Task~3, using \textbf{raw headlines} (not the Task~2 preprocessing pipeline).
DistilBERT provides its own subword tokenizer; lowercasing and stopword removal
are handled internally.
Training used learning rate $2\times10^{-5}$, batch size 16, three epochs,
weight decay 0.01, and max sequence length 128.
Checkpoints were selected by validation macro F1.
Fine-tuning ran locally on Apple Silicon in about 3.5 minutes (multiclass) and
1.4 minutes (binary); no NVIDIA GPU was used.

DistilBERT outperformed all classical models in Table~\ref{tab:results},
especially on minority classes: multiclass negative recall improved from
0.53--0.59 (MLP) to 0.90.
Remaining errors include subtle positives predicted as neutral and factual
headlines with mixed signals (e.g., acquittal or flat profit reports).
